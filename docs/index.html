Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring

A data-driven approach to prioritizing pages for content review

Abstract

This study investigates whether historical search and content signals can help prioritize webpages for content review. I constructed page-level historical features and defined a future search-performance decline proxy as the prediction target. I compared a simple baseline with a Random Forest model and evaluated the model under the original and client-grouped validation designs. The model measured a Precision@50 of 0.90 under the original split and 0.52 under client-grouped validation. The results provide directional decision-support for prioritizing page review, but they do not establish causal refresh impact or predict Google's ranking algorithm.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content teams may have many webpages but limited time to review them. This project investigates which pages should be reviewed first using historical search and content signals.

2. Data

The analysis uses the FlyRank internship warehouse accessed through DuckDB. Historical observations are used to build model features, while a later observation period is used only to define the future outcome proxy.

3. Methodology

The task is framed as ranking pages by their likelihood of the defined future decline outcome. Historical impressions, clicks, and average position are used as features. A Random Forest model is compared against a simpler baseline.

4. Validation Design

The original evaluation measured Precision@50 of 0.90. Under client-grouped validation, Precision@50 measured 0.52.

5. Results

The difference between the original and client-grouped results shows that measured performance is sensitive to validation design. The grouped result provides a more conservative estimate for unseen clients.

6. Limitations and Honest Framing

The target is a proxy for future search-performance decline rather than a direct measure of whether a page should be refreshed. The findings are observed and measured within the tested setup and should be treated as directional decision-support.

The analysis does not prove that refreshing a recommended page will cause performance to improve and does not predict Google's ranking algorithm.

7. Ranked Recommendations

The final action queue ranks pages by model score and assigns review actions. The recommendations are intended to support human prioritization.
